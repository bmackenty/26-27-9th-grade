# The five pathways

A pathway is a direction for your project, not a ranking. Today choose a first preference and a backup. These small examples are starting ideas, not the complete future project requirements.

## Computer Science — Algorithms & Logic

**Question:** How can I solve a problem correctly and efficiently?

You enjoy puzzles, tracing steps and explaining why an algorithm works.

**Small starting idea:** Compare two ways of searching five names.

**Useful components:** An input list, a table of steps, and a result card.

**Try on paper:** Write the search steps and trace them by hand.

**Remember:** A table of numbers is not a search algorithm.

## Computational Biology — Patterns in Living Systems

**Question:** What can data or a model tell me about a living system?

You enjoy biology, ecology and interpreting evidence.

**Small starting idea:** Model a fictional population changing over five days.

**Useful components:** A population input, a table of days, and a summary card.

**Try on paper:** State a simple growth rule, predict two days and identify a limitation.

**Remember:** A simulation is a simplified model, not proof of what real organisms will do.

## Computing in Business — Data, Decisions & Risk

**Question:** How can evidence help an organisation make a decision?

You enjoy economics, comparing options and checking claims.

**Small starting idea:** Compare costs and income for a fictional school stall.

**Useful components:** A sales table, a profit info box, and a recommendation callout.

**Try on paper:** Calculate one profit by hand: income minus cost.

**Remember:** A displayed profit number needs a correct calculation and trustworthy input.

## Game Programming — Systems & Feedback Loops

**Question:** How do rules and player choices create an interesting game?

You enjoy designing, playtesting and balancing choices.

**Small starting idea:** Plan a three-turn treasure game with a risk-or-bank choice.

**Useful components:** A score box, action buttons, and a rules modal.

**Try on paper:** Play one round on paper and record a rule you would improve.

**Remember:** Buttons and a score display are not yet a working game.

## Practical Computing — Automate, Work Smarter

**Question:** Which repetitive task could a program make easier?

You enjoy useful tools, testing and solving everyday frustrations.

**Small starting idea:** Plan a study-session organiser for three homework tasks.

**Useful components:** A task form, a task list, and a progress bar.

**Try on paper:** Describe the manual steps and test the plan with three invented tasks.

**Remember:** An input form does not save, prioritise or schedule tasks by itself.

## What stays the same?

All pathways involve investigating a need, designing, building, testing and explaining decisions. Your course plans two different pathway units across the year. Your first choice does not commit you to one area forever.

For this introduction, follow your teacher’s AI instructions. The Unit 2 scope uses Mode 2: explain your attempt, ask for guidance, explain the answer, predict and verify, and record AI use. The syllabus describes different longer-term AI emphases by pathway; choosing Practical Computing does not automatically grant unrestricted AI use today.
